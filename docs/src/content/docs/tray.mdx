Tray items keep their Quick Look thumbnails and sizes, drag back out to any app, and survive relaunches.

<Shot name="12-open-tray" eager alt="The Tray tab with five files on a shelf and a tile for adding more." />

## Keeping files

Drag files toward the notch and it opens with four drop zones. Let go on the one you want.

<Shot name="13-open-tray-dropping" alt="The four drop zones: Keep in Tray, AirDrop, Compress and Copy to." />

| Zone | What it does |
|---|---|
| Keep in Tray | Stashes them for later |
| AirDrop | Sends them nearby |
| Compress | Makes a .zip |
| Copy to… | Asks for a folder, and copies them there |

With an image in the drag there are two more, **Copy Text** and **Cut Out**. See [Images](#images).

Only files and images open the notch as you drag. With the Tray already open, you can drop links and text on it too, and they become files, as dropped images do. The last tile, which takes whatever room the files leave, adds files from an Open panel.

## Previewing

Click a file and press <Keys k="Space" /> to see it in Quick Look, as in Finder. The arrow keys move between files.

<Shot name="92-open-tray-selected" alt="The Tray with Hero shot.png selected." />

## What a file can do

Right-click a file for the full list:

- Quick Look, Open, Open With and Show in Finder
- Copy, AirDrop and Share
- Compress and Extract
- Copy Text and Cut Out, for images
- Convert Image to PNG, JPEG or HEIC, and Make Smaller
- Copy to…, Move to… and Remove

The toolbar above the shelf AirDrops, shares, compresses or clears everything at once.

## Images

Right-click an image for **Copy Text** and **Cut Out**, or drop one on their zones. The zones appear only while the drag holds an image. With other files in the same drag, they use just the images.

| Action | What it does |
|---|---|
| Copy Text | Puts the text in the image on the clipboard, as [Capture](/capture/#copying-text) does, QR codes included |
| Cut Out | Saves just the subject of the image, with transparency around it |

A cut-out is a PNG named `<name> (cut out).png`, beside the original, or in the Tray's folder if that folder can't be written to. It never replaces a file, and the original stays as it was. It lands in the Tray, and a notice names it, with **Open** and **Show** buttons. If nothing stands out in the picture, the notch says **No subject found** and no file is made.

An image dragged out of a browser works too. Its cut-out lands in the Tray, and the copy of the image Dynotch used to read it is removed afterwards.

## Screenshots

New screenshots and screen recordings land in the Tray as macOS saves them, from whichever folder the Screenshot app uses. Turn this off with **Add screenshots to the tray** in <Path to="Settings → Tray → Screenshots" />.

Dynotch's own [captures](/capture/) save to the same folder, so they land there too.

## Tidying

Screenshots, screen recordings and downloads sent to the Tray leave by themselves after a week, so the shelf doesn't fill up. Choose **A day**, **A week** or **Until removed** in <Path to="Settings → Tray → Tidying" />.

To keep one, right-click it and choose **Pin**. A pin shows on its tile, and it stays through tidying and through **Clear**. Anything you put in the Tray yourself stays until you remove it.

The files themselves stay where they are. Only copies the Tray made go to the Trash, as they do on **Remove from Tray**.

## Copies or originals

By default a Tray item points at the file where it is, so moving or deleting the original affects the item.

With **Keep copies** on in <Path to="Settings → Tray → Dropping files" />, the Tray keeps its own copy of every dropped file, so the originals can be moved or deleted. Copies go to the Trash when you remove them.

## Clipboard

The switch above the shelf turns it over to the last 50 things you copied as text, with pins for what you reuse. This is off until you turn it on: click **Turn On** on the Clipboard side, or use **Keep clipboard history** in <Path to="Settings → Tray → Clipboard" />.

A card shows a few lines of what you copied. A command or some code is set in monospace, a lone hex color fills a swatch, and a lone link shows its host.

- **Click a card** to copy it back and close the notch.
- **Right-click a card** for Copy, Pin or Unpin, Open Link on a web link, and Remove.
- **Search clips** finds the clips with every word you type.
- The trash button clears everything that isn't pinned. What's on the clipboard itself stays.

`clip` in the [command field](/command-field/) finds them too. Dragging files to the notch still brings up the drop zones, and a drop goes back to Files.

### What's kept

Text, up to 100,000 characters. Copying the same thing again moves it to the front, and pins don't count toward the 50.

Copies stay in memory, and they're gone when Dynotch quits or you turn the history off. Pins stay until you remove them, and they still work with the history off, though unpinning one then removes it. See [Privacy](/privacy/#clipboard).

These are left out:

- Files and images
- Anything an app marks as a password or as temporary, as **Copy Code** on a login code does
- Anything copied with a password manager in front: 1Password, Bitwarden, LastPass, Dashlane, KeePassXC, Keeper, Enpass, NordPass, Proton Pass, Passwords or Keychain Access
- What arrives from another device through Universal Clipboard
- Whatever was already on the clipboard when you turned this on, or when Dynotch started

macOS doesn't say when something's copied, so Dynotch looks when the pointer moves, when you switch apps and every few seconds. Two copies within a few seconds, with neither of those in between, keep only the second.

### If macOS asks

macOS can ask before an app reads what another app copied, so your first copy after turning this on may bring up its alert. If macOS is set to ask every time, or not to allow it, Dynotch stops reading rather than bring up an alert for each copy, and says so on the Clipboard side and in Settings.

The button beside that opens <Path to="System Settings → Privacy & Security → Paste from Other Apps" />. Set Dynotch to **Allow**. If that doesn't do it, quit Dynotch and open it again.

## Settings

| Setting | What it does | Starts as |
|---|---|---|
| Open on drag | Opens the Tray when files are dragged toward the notch | On |
| Keep copies | Keeps a copy of every dropped file | Off |
| Add screenshots to the tray | New screenshots and screen recordings show up here | On |
| Keep screenshots and downloads | How long they stay: a day, a week, or until removed | A week |
| Keep clipboard history | The last 50 things you copy, on the Clipboard side and with `clip` in the command field | Off |

**Show Folder**, **Clear Tray** and **Clear Clipboard History** are on the same page. To send finished downloads here as well, turn on **Downloads to the Tray** in <Path to="Settings → Live Activities → Options" />.

From a script, `dynotch tray` adds files. See [The dynotch command](/dynotch-command/#tray).
